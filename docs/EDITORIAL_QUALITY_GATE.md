# Documentary editorial quality gate

This standard supplements codec, licence, source-provenance and semantic checks. A technically valid MP4 is not automatically an editorially publishable documentary.

## Visuals
- Every shot must show the subject or action described by its narration.
- Search phrases and stock-library query strings are never viewer-facing graphics.
- Use real, licensed footage where it exists; use purpose-built Blender/Manim explainers where a concept needs explanation.
- Charts require structured labels and values and must be grounded in cited source material. Never use decorative bars, sine waves or network nodes as fake data.
- Reuse of an identical chart is an editorial defect. Reuse of footage must be intentional and reviewed.
- Chapter titles should be brief overlays on relevant moving imagery, not long blank slates.
- AI reconstructions and conceptual diagrams must be visibly disclosed as such and must not be presented as historical evidence.

## Captions
- Documentary captions use documentary reading limits, not Shorts limits.
- Prefer one or two complete lines per cue; balance line lengths and avoid isolated two-to-four-word fragments.
- Captions must be readable at 1080p, synchronized to narration and English-only for the English track.

## Audio
- Master target: -14 LUFS integrated, -1 dBTP ceiling, approximately 9 LU target loudness range.
- Background ambience should remain roughly 15–20 dB below narration and be ducked while speech is present.
- If no cleared music track is available, the renderer may generate a quiet original ambient drone; this is not a substitute for human listening review.
- Human review must listen to the complete export. Measurements alone cannot certify speech intelligibility or musical taste.

## Release
- The fallback-card limit, semantic mismatch gate, source/licence gate and private-only YouTube upload behavior remain in force.
- A failed or missing report is blocking. Do not lower thresholds or set fallback bypass flags to make a build pass.
- Human reviewers must inspect representative full-resolution frames, source rights, factual claims, presenter disclosure and the final audio.
